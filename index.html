Welcome to Module 2. In this module, you will learn how to use common digital devices confidently and safely.

Lesson 1: Understanding Digital Devices

Digital devices are electronic tools that allow us to create, store, process and share information. Examples include smartphones, tablets, laptops and desktop computers.

Module 3: Internet and Web Basics

Welcome to Module 3. In this module, you will learn how the internet works and how to use the web effectively.

Lesson 1: Understanding the Internet

The internet is a global network that connects computers, smartphones and other digital devices. It allows people to communicate, learn, work and share information online.

Lesson 2: Web Browsers and Websites

A web browser is an application used to access websites on the internet. Examples include Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Mozilla Firefox and Safari.

Lesson 3: Searching for Information Online

Search engines help users find information online. You can improve your search results by using clear and specific keywords.

Practical Exercise

Module 4: Digital Communication

Welcome to Module 4. In this module, you will learn how to communicate effectively and professionally using digital tools.

Lesson 1: Understanding Digital Communication

Digital communication is the exchange of information through digital devices and online platforms. Examples include email, WhatsApp, social media, video calls and messaging applications.

Lesson 2: Email Communication

Email is an important communication tool used for personal, educational and professional purposes. A good email should have a clear subject, polite greeting, useful message and appropriate closing.

Lesson 3: Professional Online Communication

Professional communication online should be clear, respectful and easy to understand. Avoid unnecessary words, offensive language and sharing private information with strangers.

Practical Exercise

Open your email application and practice writing a professional email to a teacher, customer or potential employer. Include a clear subject, greeting, short message and polite closing.

Module 5: Digital Safety and Cybersecurity

Welcome to Module 5. In this module, you will learn how to protect yourself, your devices and your personal information while using digital technology.

Lesson 1: Understanding Digital Safety

Digital safety means using technology and the internet in a responsible and secure way. It includes protecting your personal information, accounts, devices and online identity.

Lesson 2: Creating Strong Passwords

A strong password should be difficult for other people to guess. Use a combination of letters, numbers and symbols. Avoid using easily guessed information such as your name, birthday or phone number.

Lesson 3: Recognizing Online Scams

Online scams are attempts to trick people into giving away money, passwords or personal information. Be careful with suspicious messages, unexpected links, fake offers and requests for confidential information.

Lesson 4: Protecting Personal Information

Personal information such as passwords, bank details, identification numbers and private account information should be protected. Do not share sensitive information with people or websites you do not trust.

Practical Exercise

Review the passwords you use for your online accounts. Identify any weak or reused passwords and create stronger, unique passwords for your important accounts.

Quiz

  1. What does digital safety mean?
  2. What makes a password strong?
  3. What is an online scam?
  4. Name two types of personal information that should be protected.
  5. Why should you be careful when clicking unknown links?

Assignment

Write five rules you will follow to stay safe while using the internet and digital devices.

Lesson 1: Understanding Productivity Tools

Digital productivity tools are applications and online services that help you complete tasks faster and more efficiently. Examples include word processors, spreadsheets, calendars, note-taking applications and cloud storage services.

Lesson 2: Word Processing

Word processing tools allow you to create, edit, format and save documents. They can be used for writing letters, reports, assignments, CVs and other documents.

Lesson 3: Spreadsheets

Spreadsheets are useful for organizing information in rows and columns. They can also be used to perform calculations, create simple budgets and manage data.

Lesson 4: Cloud Storage

Cloud storage allows you to save files online and access them from different devices. It can also help you keep important files backed up and organized.

Module 7: Digital Content Creation

Welcome to Module 7. In this module, you will learn the basics of creating useful and engaging digital content.

Lesson 1: Understanding Digital Content

Digital content is information created and shared in digital formats. Examples include images, videos, articles, graphics, social media posts and audio.

Lesson 2: Choosing Content Ideas

Good content should provide value to the people who see it. Content ideas can come from questions people ask, problems they want to solve, personal experiences, education and current interests.

Lesson 3: Creating Simple Visual Content

Visual content can be created using smartphones and simple design applications. Good visual content should be clear, readable and attractive.

Lesson 4: Creating Short Videos

Short videos can be used to educate, entertain, promote a product or share information. A good short video should have a clear idea, simple message and engaging presentation.

Module 8: Social Media Skills

Welcome to Module 8. In this module, you will learn how to use social media platforms effectively for communication, learning, networking and business.

Lesson 1: Understanding Social Media

Social media platforms allow people to create, share and interact with content online. Examples include Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X and WhatsApp.

Lesson 2: Creating a Professional Profile

A professional social media profile should clearly communicate who you are and what you do. Use a clear profile picture, appropriate name, useful description and relevant information.

Lesson 3: Building an Online Presence

Building an online presence requires consistency and useful content. Share information that is relevant to your audience and communicate with people respectfully.

Social media can help businesses promote products and services, communicate with customers, build trust and reach new audiences. Businesses should provide useful information and avoid misleading claims.

Module 9: Online Business and Freelancing

Welcome to Module 9. In this module, you will learn the basics of using digital skills to provide services and explore online business opportunities.

Lesson 1: Understanding Online Business

An online business provides products or services through the internet. Examples include selling digital products, offering online services, teaching, content creation and freelancing.

Lesson 2: Identifying Your Digital Skill

Before starting an online service, identify a skill you can develop and offer to other people. Examples include graphic design, writing, social media management, virtual assistance, video editing and content creation.

Lesson 3: Introduction to Freelancing

Freelancing means offering your skills or services to clients without being permanently employed by one organization. Freelancers may work with clients locally or internationally.

Lesson 4: Finding Clients

Clients can be found through professional networking, social media, referrals, online marketplaces and direct communication. Always communicate professionally and clearly explain the service you provide.

Practical Exercise

Module 10: AI and Digital Tools

Welcome to Module 10. In this module, you will learn the basics of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and how AI-powered digital tools can support learning, creativity, productivity and online work.

Lesson 1: Understanding Artificial Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence, commonly called AI, is technology that allows computers and digital systems to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. These tasks can include understanding language, generating content, recognizing images and assisting with problem-solving.

Lesson 2: Examples of AI Tools

AI is now available through many digital tools and applications. Examples include AI writing assistants, image-generation tools, video tools, translation tools, research assistants and productivity applications.

Lesson 3: Using AI Effectively

To use AI effectively, you should give clear instructions and provide enough information about what you want. These instructions are often called prompts.

A good prompt should clearly explain the task, the information needed, the desired format and, when necessary, the audience or purpose.

Lesson 4: Responsible Use of AI

AI should be used responsibly. Always review AI-generated information before relying on it, especially when accuracy is important. Avoid entering sensitive personal information into AI tools and do not use AI to deceive, plagiarize or harm others.

Practical Exercise

Choose one AI tool and use it to help you complete a simple task, such as generating ideas, summarizing information, creating a study plan or improving a piece of writing.

Quiz

  1. What does AI stand for?
  2. What is Artificial Intelligence?
  3. Name three types of tasks AI tools can help with.
  4. What is a prompt?
  5. Why should AI-generated information be reviewed?
  6. Why should sensitive personal information be protected when using AI tools?

Assignment

Choose one AI tool and create three different prompts for a useful task. Record the prompts and briefly explain what each prompt was designed to achieve.

Module 11: Building Your Digital Portfolio

Welcome to Module 11. In this module, you will learn how to organize and present your digital skills and work in a simple professional portfolio.

Lesson 1: What Is a Digital Portfolio?

A digital portfolio is a collection of your work, skills, achievements and projects that you can show to potential clients, employers or other people who may be interested in your abilities.

Lesson 2: What to Include in a Portfolio

A beginner portfolio can include a short introduction, your skills, examples of your work, projects you have completed and your contact information.

Lesson 3: Creating Examples of Your Work

If you are still a beginner and do not have clients, you can create sample projects to demonstrate your abilities. For example, you can create a sample social media design, written article, video, presentation or spreadsheet.

Lesson 4: Presenting Yourself Professionally

Your portfolio should be clear, organized and easy to understand. Use professional language and make sure your contact information is accurate.

Practical Exercise

Create a simple list of your digital skills and choose three pieces of work that you would like to include in your portfolio. If you do not have existing work, create three simple sample projects.

Quiz

  1. What is a digital portfolio?
  2. Who can benefit from having a digital portfolio?
  3. Name three things that can be included in a portfolio.
  4. What can beginners use if they do not have client work?
  5. Why should a portfolio be organized and professional?

Assignment

Create a basic digital portfolio containing your introduction, at least three digital skills and three examples of your work. Save your portfolio so you can update it as your skills improve.

Module 12: Digital Career Development

Welcome to Module 12. In this final module, you will learn how to use your digital skills to prepare for future opportunities, continue learning and develop a sustainable digital career.

Lesson 1: Understanding Digital Career Opportunities

Digital skills can be used in many areas of work and business. Opportunities may include freelancing, remote work, digital marketing, content creation, graphic design, virtual assistance, online education and other technology-related services.

Lesson 2: Choosing Your Digital Path

Everyone has different interests and strengths. Think about the digital skills you enjoy learning and the type of problems you would like to solve. Choose one or two areas to develop further instead of trying to master everything at once.

Lesson 3: Continuous Learning

Digital technology changes regularly, so learning should not stop after completing this course. Continue practicing your skills, exploring new tools and learning from reliable educational resources.

Lesson 4: Building a Personal Action Plan

A digital career requires clear goals and consistent action. Create a simple plan that identifies the skill you want to develop, what you will practice, how you will build experience and how you will look for opportunities.

Practical Exercise

Choose one digital skill you want to develop over the next three months. Write down what you want to achieve, the resources you will use to learn, how often you will practice and one practical project you will complete.

Quiz

  1. Name three digital career opportunities.
  2. Why is it useful to choose a specific digital skill to develop?
  3. Why should digital learning continue after completing a course?
  4. What should be included in a digital career action plan?
  5. Why is consistent practice important?

Final Assignment

Create a three-month Digital Career Action Plan. Include the digital skill you want to develop, your learning goals, your weekly practice schedule, one portfolio project and how you plan to look for opportunities after completing this course.
