Module 1: Digital Skills Foundations

Welcome to Module 1 of the Beginner Digital Skills Course. This module introduces you to the basic digital skills you need for learning, communication, work, business and everyday life.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Explain what digital skills are.
  • Identify common digital devices and tools.
  • Explain why digital skills are important.
  • Identify digital skills that can be useful for work and business.
  • Describe how you can begin improving your digital skills.

Lesson 1: Introduction to Digital Skills

Digital skills are the knowledge and ability to use digital devices, applications, software and online platforms effectively.

In simple terms, digital skills mean knowing how to use technology to perform useful tasks.

For example, when you use a smartphone to send a WhatsApp message, search for information on Google, create a document, edit a picture or join an online class, you are using digital skills.

Examples of Digital Skills

  • Using a smartphone or computer.
  • Browsing the Internet.
  • Searching for information online.
  • Sending and receiving emails.
  • Creating documents.
  • Using social media.
  • Creating digital content.
  • Using online communication tools.
  • Protecting your accounts and personal information.
  • Using AI and other digital tools responsibly.

Simple Example

Imagine that you want to advertise a small business online. You may use your smartphone to take a product photograph, Canva to create a poster, WhatsApp to communicate with customers and social media to promote the product.

Several digital skills are being used together to achieve one goal.

Lesson 2: Why Digital Skills Are Important

Technology is now part of many areas of everyday life. People use digital tools for education, communication, business, employment, entertainment and accessing information.

Developing digital skills can help you become more confident when using technology and can create opportunities for learning and work.

1. Education

Students can use the Internet, educational websites, online courses, digital documents and learning applications to improve their knowledge.

2. Communication

Digital tools make it possible to communicate with people through email, messaging applications, video calls and social media.

3. Work

Many jobs require employees to use computers, smartphones, email, online meeting platforms and other digital tools.

4. Business

Business owners can use digital tools to advertise products, communicate with customers, create content and manage parts of their businesses.

5. Career Opportunities

Digital skills can help people explore opportunities such as content creation, graphic design, social media management, virtual assistance, freelancing and other digital careers.

Lesson 3: Common Digital Devices

A digital device is an electronic device that can process, store or communicate digital information.

Common examples include:

  • Smartphones
  • Computers
  • Laptops
  • Tablets
  • Smart televisions
  • Digital cameras

Different devices can perform different tasks, but many modern devices can connect to the Internet and run applications.

Smartphone

A smartphone can be used for communication, photography, Internet browsing, learning, banking, social media, content creation and many other activities.

Computer

A computer can be used for creating documents, working with spreadsheets, browsing the Internet, editing media, programming and many other tasks.

Lesson 4: Common Digital Tools

Digital tools are applications, software or online services that help people complete specific tasks.

Examples include:

  • Web browsers for accessing websites.
  • Email applications for communication.
  • Word processors for creating documents.
  • Spreadsheets for organizing information and calculations.
  • Cloud storage for storing files online.
  • Design applications for creating visual content.
  • Video editing applications for creating videos.
  • AI tools for assisting with certain digital tasks.

You do not need to master every digital tool at once. Start with the tools that are most useful for your goals and gradually learn more.

Lesson 5: Building Your Digital Skills

Digital skills improve through learning and practice. You do not need to know everything before you begin.

Step 1 — Choose a Skill

Start with one useful skill. For example, you could begin with creating documents, designing simple graphics or using email professionally.

Step 2 — Learn the Basics

Study the basic concepts and learn how the tool works. Do not worry about becoming an expert immediately.

Step 3 — Practice

Use the skill repeatedly. Create small projects that allow you to apply what you have learned.

Step 4 — Improve

Look at your work, identify mistakes and improve it. Each practice session can help you become more confident.

Step 5 — Apply the Skill

Once you become comfortable with a skill, look for ways to use it in school, work, business or personal projects.

Real-Life Practice Example

Suppose you want to help a small business promote its products.

You could use your smartphone to take product photographs, a design application to create a promotional poster and WhatsApp or social media to communicate with potential customers.

This simple example shows how several digital skills can work together to solve a real-world problem.

Welcome to Module 2 of the Beginner Digital Skills Course. In this module, you will learn how to use common digital devices confidently, safely and effectively.

Lesson 1: Understanding Digital Devices

A digital device is an electronic device that can create, process, store, receive or share digital information.

Digital devices are now part of everyday life. People use them for communication, education, entertainment, business, work and accessing information.

Examples of Digital Devices

  • Smartphones
  • Tablets
  • Laptops
  • Desktop computers
  • Smart televisions
  • Digital cameras
  • Smart watches

Why Do We Use Digital Devices?

Digital devices help us perform many tasks faster and more conveniently.

For example, a smartphone can help you:

  • Make phone calls.
  • Send messages.
  • Browse the Internet.
  • Take photographs and videos.
  • Attend online classes.
  • Use banking applications.
  • Create digital content.
  • Communicate with customers.

Lesson 2: Understanding Your Smartphone

A smartphone is one of the most useful digital devices available today. It combines communication, Internet access, photography, applications and many other functions in one device.

Important Smartphone Features

  • Touchscreen
  • Camera
  • Microphone
  • Speaker
  • Internet connection
  • Storage
  • Applications
  • Settings

Learning what these features do will help you use your smartphone more confidently.

Lesson 3: Basic Smartphone Settings

The Settings application allows you to control many functions of your smartphone.

Depending on your device, Settings may allow you to manage:

  • Wi-Fi
  • Mobile data
  • Bluetooth
  • Display and brightness
  • Sound and volume
  • Notifications
  • Security
  • Applications
  • Storage
  • Battery

Practical Example

If your phone is connected to the wrong Wi-Fi network, you can open Settings, select Wi-Fi and choose the correct available network.

Lesson 4: Connecting to Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi allows compatible devices to connect to the Internet through a wireless network.

Basic Steps

  1. Open your phone's Settings.
  2. Select Wi-Fi or Network & Internet.
  3. Turn Wi-Fi on if it is disabled.
  4. Choose an available network.
  5. Enter the password if required.
  6. Wait for the device to connect.

Only connect to networks you trust. Be careful when using unfamiliar public Wi-Fi networks, especially when handling sensitive information.

Lesson 5: Installing and Managing Applications

Applications, often called apps, are software programs designed to perform specific tasks on a digital device.

Examples include messaging apps, learning apps, banking apps, social media apps, design apps and productivity apps.

Before Installing an App

  • Use an official app store where possible.
  • Check the application name and developer.
  • Read reviews carefully.
  • Check what permissions the app requests.
  • Avoid suspicious applications.

Managing Applications

You can usually update, remove or manage applications through your device's settings or app store.

Lesson 6: Managing Files and Folders

Files are digital items such as photographs, videos, documents, PDFs and audio recordings.

Folders help you organize related files so that they are easier to find.

Examples of Files

  • Photos
  • Videos
  • PDF documents
  • Word documents
  • Audio recordings
  • Downloaded files

Good File Management Practices

  • Give files clear names.
  • Keep related files in appropriate folders.
  • Delete files you no longer need.
  • Back up important files.
  • Be careful before deleting important information.

Lesson 7: Taking Screenshots

A screenshot is an image of what is currently displayed on your device's screen.

Screenshots can be useful when you want to save information, demonstrate a problem or show someone what is displayed on your screen.

The method for taking a screenshot depends on the device. On many smartphones, pressing the Power button and Volume Down button at the same time takes a screenshot.

Your device may use a different method, so check your device instructions if necessary.

Lesson 8: Smartphone Storage

Storage is the space available on your device for saving applications, photographs, videos, documents and other files.

When storage becomes full, your device may become difficult to use and may not allow you to save new files or install applications.

Ways to Manage Storage

  • Delete unnecessary files.
  • Remove applications you no longer use.
  • Move important files to suitable storage.
  • Review large videos and duplicate files.
  • Back up important information before deleting it.

Lesson 9: Keeping Your Device Safe

Your digital device can contain personal information, photographs, messages, documents and account information. Protecting the device is therefore very important.

Basic Safety Practices

  • Use a screen lock.
  • Keep your device software updated.
  • Install applications carefully.
  • Do not share your device password unnecessarily.
  • Avoid suspicious links and downloads.
  • Back up important information.
  • Be careful when lending your device to others.

Welcome to Module 3 of the Beginner Digital Skills Course. In this module, you will learn how the internet works, how websites are accessed, how to search for information, and how to use online information responsibly.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

Lesson 1: Understanding the Internet

The internet is a worldwide network that connects millions of computers, smartphones, tablets, servers and other digital devices.

Through the internet, people can communicate, learn, work, shop, conduct business, watch videos, access services and share information with people around the world.

For example, when you send a WhatsApp message, watch a YouTube video or visit a website, your device uses an internet connection to communicate with other computers and online services.

What Can You Do on the Internet?

  • Send and receive messages.
  • Make voice and video calls.
  • Search for information.
  • Attend online classes.
  • Apply for jobs and opportunities.
  • Promote a business.
  • Buy and sell products and services.
  • Store and share information.
  • Access online banking and other digital services.

Lesson 2: Internet Connection

Your device needs an internet connection before you can access most online services.

Common ways of connecting to the internet include mobile data, Wi-Fi and broadband connections.

Mobile Data

Mobile data allows your smartphone or other supported device to connect to the internet through a mobile network.

Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi allows a device to connect to the internet through a wireless network. Wi-Fi can be available at home, school, offices, hotels, libraries and other locations.

Important Tip

Always check your internet connection before assuming that a website or application is not working.

Lesson 3: Understanding the Web

The World Wide Web, commonly called the web, is a collection of websites and web pages that people can access through the internet.

The internet and the web are related, but they are not exactly the same thing. The internet is the larger network that connects devices, while the web is one of the services that operates through that network.

For example, when you open a website using Chrome, you are using the web through an internet connection.

Lesson 4: Web Browsers

A web browser is an application that allows you to access and view websites on the internet.

Common web browsers include:

  • Google Chrome
  • Microsoft Edge
  • Mozilla Firefox
  • Safari
  • Opera

Most smartphones already have a web browser installed. You can open the browser and enter a website address or search for information.

Example

If you want to visit a website, you can type its web address into the browser's address bar and press Enter.

Lesson 5: Websites and Web Pages

A website is a collection of related web pages that are available online under a particular web address.

A web page is an individual page within a website.

For example, an online academy may have separate pages for the home page, courses, registration, contact information and student resources.

Web Address

A web address, also called a URL, is the address used to locate a particular page or website on the internet.

Lesson 6: Search Engines

A search engine is an online service that helps users find information on the internet.

Examples include Google, Bing and other search services.

Instead of visiting websites one by one, you can enter a question or keywords into a search engine and receive relevant results.

Example

Instead of searching for:

digital

You could make your search more specific:

beginner digital skills course

The second search gives the search engine more information about what you are looking for.

Lesson 7: Using Effective Search Keywords

Keywords are the important words you enter into a search engine to find information.

Good keywords should be clear, specific and related to the information you need.

Example

If you want to learn how to create a CV, searching for:

how to create a professional CV for beginners

is more useful than simply searching for:

CV

Search Tip

If your first search does not give you useful results, change your keywords and try again.

Lesson 8: Checking Information Online

Not everything you see on the internet is accurate or trustworthy. Before using important information, you should check where it came from.

Look at the website, the author, the date of publication and whether other reliable sources provide similar information.

Questions to Ask

  • Who published this information?
  • When was it published or updated?
  • Does the website appear trustworthy?
  • Can the information be confirmed from another reliable source?
  • Is the information trying to make me click, pay or share something?

Lesson 9: Safe and Responsible Internet Use

Using the internet effectively also means using it safely and responsibly.

  • Do not share passwords with strangers.
  • Be careful when clicking unknown links.
  • Do not share sensitive personal information unnecessarily.
  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Be careful when downloading files.
  • Check information before sharing it with others.
  • Respect other people when communicating online.

Internet safety will be studied in greater detail in Module 5: Digital Safety and Cybersecurity.

Module 5: Digital Safety and Cybersecurity

Welcome to Module 5 of the Beginner Digital Skills Course. In this module, you will learn how to protect yourself, your devices, accounts and personal information while using digital technology and the internet.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  • Explain what digital safety means.
  • Understand basic cybersecurity principles.
  • Create stronger and safer passwords.
  • Understand why two-factor authentication is important.
  • Recognize common online scams.
  • Identify suspicious links and messages.
  • Protect personal and financial information.
  • Use public Wi-Fi more safely.
  • Keep devices and applications updated.
  • Respond appropriately when an account or device is compromised.

Lesson 1: Understanding Digital Safety

Digital safety means taking steps to protect yourself, your devices, your accounts and your information when using technology and online services.

Cybersecurity is closely related to digital safety. It involves protecting computers, smartphones, networks, accounts and information from unauthorized access, attacks and other digital threats.

Why Digital Safety Matters

Many important activities now happen online. People use digital services for communication, education, shopping, business, banking and entertainment.

If an account or device is not properly protected, criminals may attempt to steal information, money or access to online accounts.

Lesson 2: Creating Strong Passwords

A password is used to help protect an account from unauthorized access.

A strong password should be difficult for other people to guess. Avoid using information that someone could easily discover, such as your name, birthday, phone number or simple words.

Good Password Practices

  • Use long passwords or passphrases.
  • Use different passwords for important accounts.
  • Avoid obvious personal information.
  • Never share your password unnecessarily.
  • Change a password if you believe it has been exposed.

For important accounts, consider using a reputable password manager to help create and store unique passwords.

Lesson 3: Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication, commonly called 2FA, adds an extra layer of security to an online account.

Instead of relying only on a password, the account may require another verification method, such as a code sent to a trusted device or an authentication application.

If someone discovers your password, the additional security step can make it more difficult for them to access your account.

Practical Tip

Where available, enable two-factor authentication on your important accounts, especially accounts containing personal, financial or business information.

Lesson 4: Recognizing Online Scams

An online scam is an attempt to deceive someone into giving away money, personal information, passwords, account access or other valuable information.

Scammers may contact people through email, SMS, social media, messaging applications, websites or phone calls.

Common Warning Signs

  • A message creates unnecessary urgency or panic.
  • You are promised an unrealistic reward or profit.
  • Someone asks for your password or security code.
  • You are asked to send money unexpectedly.
  • A link looks suspicious or does not match the expected website.
  • You are pressured to keep a transaction secret.
  • The message contains unusual spelling or suspicious details.

Remember: if an offer looks too good to be true, stop and verify it before taking action.

Lesson 5: Phishing and Suspicious Links

Phishing is a type of online fraud in which someone tries to trick you into providing sensitive information or clicking a harmful link.

A phishing message may pretend to come from a bank, social media company, delivery service, employer or another organization.

Before Clicking a Link

  1. Think about whether you were expecting the message.
  2. Check who sent it.
  3. Look carefully at the link or website address.
  4. Do not enter passwords or financial information on a suspicious page.
  5. When in doubt, visit the organization's official website directly.

Lesson 6: Protecting Personal Information

Personal information is information that relates to you or can be used to identify or contact you.

Examples include:

  • Full name
  • Home address
  • Phone number
  • Email address
  • Passwords
  • Banking information
  • Identification information
  • Private photographs and documents

You should think carefully before sharing personal information online. Only provide sensitive information when it is genuinely necessary and you are confident that the recipient or service is legitimate.

Lesson 7: Keeping Your Device Secure

Your smartphone or computer contains valuable information, so protecting the device itself is important.

  • Use a screen lock such as a PIN or password.
  • Keep your operating system updated.
  • Install applications from trusted sources.
  • Remove applications you no longer need.
  • Back up important information.
  • Do not ignore important security warnings.

Lesson 8: Safe Use of Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi can be convenient in places such as hotels, restaurants, airports and other public locations.

However, you should be cautious when using networks that you do not control.

  • Connect only to networks you trust.
  • Avoid entering sensitive information on suspicious networks.
  • Confirm the correct network name before connecting.
  • Turn off automatic connection to unknown Wi-Fi networks.
  • Disconnect from public Wi-Fi when you no longer need it.

Lesson 9: What to Do If You Suspect an Account Has Been Compromised

If you believe someone has accessed your account without permission, act quickly.

  1. Change the account password immediately.
  2. Sign out of unfamiliar devices or sessions if the service allows it.
  3. Enable two-factor authentication.
  4. Check for unusual account activity.
  5. Contact the service provider if necessary.
  6. If financial information may be affected, contact your bank or financial institution.

Never ignore signs of unauthorized access.

Real-Life Practice Example

Imagine that you receive a message saying: "Congratulations! You have won a large cash prize. Click this link immediately and enter your bank information to receive your money."

Instead of clicking the link immediately, stop and examine the message.

Ask yourself:

  • Did I enter any competition?
  • Who sent the message?
  • Is the link trustworthy?
  • Why are they asking for my bank information?
  • Can I verify the offer independently?

This type of thinking can help you avoid many online scams.

    Welcome to Digital Growth Academy and congratulations on beginning your journey into the world of digital skills.

    The Beginner Digital Skills Course introduces students to essential digital knowledge and practical skills needed for learning, communication, online work, content creation and digital career development.

    The course contains 12 learning modules followed by a Final Examination and Final Practical Assessment.

    1. Digital Skills Foundations
    2. Using Digital Devices
    3. Internet and Web Basics
    4. Digital Communication
    5. Digital Safety and Cybersecurity
    6. Digital Productivity Tools

    10. AI and Digital Tools

    3. How to Study

    • Study each module carefully.
    • Read the lesson notes before attempting the activities.
    • Complete every practical exercise.
    • Answer the quizzes honestly.
    • Complete the assignments.
    • Practice what you learn.
    • Ask for help when you do not understand something.

    4. Practical Learning

    Digital skills are best learned through practice. Students are encouraged to use their smartphone or computer to practice the skills taught in each module.

    Do not be afraid of making mistakes. Practice, correction and repetition are important parts of learning.

    5. Assignments

    Assignments are designed to help students apply the knowledge they have learned.

    Students should complete their assignments honestly and submit their own work.

    6. Final Examination

    After completing the 12 modules, students are required to complete the Final Examination.

    The examination contains 30 questions and has a pass mark of 60%.

    7. Final Practical Assessment

    Students who successfully complete the Final Examination proceed to the Final Practical Assessment.

    The practical assessment tests the student's ability to apply the digital skills learned during the course.

    The practical assessment has a pass mark of 60%.

    8. Certificate of Completion

    Students who successfully complete the required course work, pass the Final Examination and pass the Final Practical Assessment may receive a Digital Growth Academy Certificate of Completion.

    Certificate information should be verified by the academy before the certificate is issued.
